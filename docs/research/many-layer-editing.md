# How other EDA tools handle working on many copper Layers

Ticket: openreda/reda#322 · Sources read: 2026-11-18

Research run on the Codex CLI and reviewed by a Claude agent, which re-checked a sample of claims against their sources on 2026-11-18. The classic-editor training manual of a second commercial suite and its 17.2 technical guide are vendor documents read from third-party hosts (a parts distributor and a reseller); they are treated as primary but their hosting is not first-party. The KiCad findings marked 10.0.0 that cite source files describe behaviour read from the released source, not from the manual.

The leading suite keeps visible Layers in a strip along the bottom of the canvas and scrolls the strip when it fills. KiCad keeps one active-Layer selector in the top toolbar and a scrollable list alongside the board. A second commercial suite separates the floating visibility toolbar from the controls that choose the routing Layer. [The leading suite’s board-view guide](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/your-view-of-the-board) · [KiCad’s PCB Editor manual](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html) · [KiCad’s Appearance controls](https://docs.kicad.org/doxygen/appearance__controls__base_8cpp_source.html) · [Release notes for a second commercial suite](https://www.flowcad.de/datasheets/What-is-New-in-Release-23-1.pdf) · [Routing controls in its classic editor](https://www.jotrin.com/userfiles/downloadfile/201812111502274655.pdf)

The distinction with the greatest consequence for an already-routed board is between **removing a Layer** and **moving its copper**. In KiCad 10.0.0, removing the endpoint Layer of a blind Via deletes the Via, while explicitly mapping that Layer to another with **Swap Layers** can move the endpoint. [KiCad’s Layer-removal implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KiCad/kicad-source-mirror/10.0.0/pcbnew/board.cpp) · [KiCad’s Swap Layers implementation](https://gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad/-/raw/10.0.0/pcbnew/tools/global_edit_tool.cpp)

## 1. The Layer control at 4 to 16 copper Layers

| Tool | Selecting a Layer and handling overflow |
|---|---|
| the leading suite | The bottom strip has a colour swatch and name for each **visible** Layer. Clicking a name makes that Layer active for placement. When the entries exceed the available width, arrows scroll the strip horizontally. [Board-view guide](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/your-view-of-the-board) |
| KiCad 10 | The top-toolbar dropdown shows the active Layer. The Appearance panel also lists the Layers vertically, with an arrow beside the active one. Clicking the name selects it, while clicking the eye changes visibility. [Board Layers and Appearance](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html#board_layers) The panel scrolls vertically when its rows exceed the available height. [Appearance panel implementation](https://docs.kicad.org/doxygen/appearance__controls__base_8cpp_source.html) |
| a second commercial suite | The newer editor has a floating Layer visibility toolbar and a Visibility panel with controls by Layer and object type. [Release notes, “User-Defined Views”](https://www.flowcad.de/datasheets/What-is-New-in-Release-23-1.pdf) The toolbar can run horizontally at the bottom or vertically at the side, and clicking a Layer there toggles its visibility (secondary source, not verified). [Interface walkthrough](https://www.ema-eda.com/courses/orcad-x-presto-walk-through/lessons/getting-started-with-orcad-x-presto/) The classic editor identifies the routing Layer in the **Active** field of the Options panel. [Classic routing manual, lesson 10](https://www.jotrin.com/userfiles/downloadfile/201812111502274655.pdf) |

For the leading suite, the point at which scrolling starts depends on the available width and the visible Layer names, including non-copper Layers. KiCad can keep a Layer active even while its eye is off, so the highlighted row and the eye answer different questions. [The leading suite’s strip](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/your-view-of-the-board) · [KiCad’s selection and visibility controls](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html#board_layers)

## 2. Default inner-Layer colours and names

| Tool | Names, colours and treatment of power Layers |
|---|---|
| the leading suite | The documentation's examples (not a statement of defaults) use `Mid-Layer 1`, `Mid-Layer 2`, and so on for inner signal Layers, and `Internal Plane 1`, `Internal Plane 2`, and so on for dedicated internal power Layers. The user can rename either and assign a Net to an internal power Layer. [Board-view guide](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/your-view-of-the-board) A published four-copper-Layer example shows the first inner signal Layer in gold and the second in cyan. [Illustrated Layer example](https://files.resources.altium.com/sites/default/files/uberflip_docs/file_812.pdf) The current editor takes its 2D colours from an installation-wide profile shared by PCB Documents. The example establishes the two illustrated colours, but a complete current factory palette was not verified. [Colour-profile preferences](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/preferences/pcb-editor) |
| KiCad 10 | The canonical names run from `F.Cu` through `In1.Cu`, `In2.Cu`, and the remaining numbered inner Layers to `B.Cu`. [KiCad file-format specification](https://dev-docs.kicad.org/en/file-formats/sexpr-intro/) In the **KiCad Default** theme, the first four inner Layers are soft green, orange-brown, turquoise and rose, respectively. Some colours recur higher in the Stackup, including the same colour for `In5.Cu` and `In13.Cu`. [KiCad 10.0.0 built-in themes](https://gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad/-/raw/10.0.0/common/settings/builtin_color_themes.h) The user can rename Layers and designate them as signal, power plane, mixed or jumper. Tracks and Zones are permitted on all four types. [Board Editor Layers](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html#board_setup) |
| a second commercial suite | The classic editor starts with `TOP` and `BOTTOM`. It distinguishes conductor and plane types and requires a unique name for each; two ground Layers can be named `GND1` and `GND2`. [Classic Stackup manual, lesson 5](https://www.jotrin.com/userfiles/downloadfile/201812111502274655.pdf) In a newer-editor walkthrough, adding a conductor produces `LAYER_1`, supplying `L2_GND` as the plane-name prefix produces `L2_GND_1`, and newly added Layers initially appear dark green (secondary source, not verified). [Stackup walkthrough, steps 4–19](https://www.ema-eda.com/how-to-page/how-to-add-layers-to-a-pcb-stackup-in-orcad/) |

A Layer named `GND` can mean different things electrically. In the leading suite’s dedicated plane type, a line cuts an insulating gap in the copper. In KiCad, setting the Layer type to power plane still permits an ordinary Track, and a Net-assigned Zone supplies the filled copper. [The leading suite’s internal power Layer guide](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/internal-power-split-planes) · [KiCad’s copper Layer types and Zones](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html)

## 3. Routing Layer pairs and Layer-switching keys

| Tool | Switching the active Layer | Changing Layer during a route |
|---|---|---|
| the leading suite | Keypad `+` and `−` select the next and previous enabled Layers. Keypad `*` and `Shift`+keypad `*` step forward and backward through enabled **signal** Layers. `Ctrl`+`Shift`+mouse wheel also steps through enabled Layers. [PCB editor shortcuts](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/shortcut-keys/pcb-editors) | During routing, keypad `+` and `−` step through signal Layers, keypad `1`–`9` jump to a Layer, and `Ctrl`+`L` opens the destination list. Changing Layer inserts a Via under the applicable routing rule. [Interactive routing guide](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/routing/interactive) With the Via on the cursor, `6` cycles through suitable Via types or stacks and `8` lists them. `2` adds a Via without changing Layer, and `/` adds a fanout Via and waits for the next fanout. [Via placement guide](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/pads-vias) · [PCB editor shortcuts](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/shortcut-keys/pcb-editors) |
| KiCad 10 | `+` and `−` step to the next and previous Layers. `PgUp` selects `F.Cu`, `PgDn` selects `B.Cu`, and `V` toggles between the active routing pair. Direct actions for inner Layers 1–30 have no default bindings in the shortcut list. [PCB Editor shortcut reference](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html) | `V` starts a through Via to the pair partner, except that a ratsnest connection to an item on a different Layer can select that destination instead. Click to fix the Via and continue routing. `<` opens a destination chooser, `Ctrl`+`V` starts a microvia, and `Alt`+`Shift`+`V` starts a blind or buried Via. [Placing Vias](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html#placing_vias) The two-colour selector stores named, enabled routing pairs in the project, and `Shift`+`V` cycles those presets. [Layer pairs](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html#layer_pairs) |
| a second commercial suite | The classic editor has **Active** and **Alternate** routing Layers. **Swap** exchanges them. [Classic routing manual, lesson 10](https://www.jotrin.com/userfiles/downloadfile/201812111502274655.pdf) Its 17.2 guide shows how to bind `+` and `−` for stepping and digits for named Layers. These are examples of user bindings. [Keyboard configuration, p. 30](https://www.flowcad.de/AN/flowcad-cadence-AN-allegro-tips.pdf#page=30) | Adding a Via in classic Active/Alternate mode exchanges the two Layers. **Working Layer** mode instead offers a list of enabled destinations at the Via. [Classic routing manual](https://www.jotrin.com/userfiles/downloadfile/201812111502274655.pdf) · [Working Layer guide, p. 22](https://www.flowcad.de/AN/flowcad-cadence-AN-allegro-tips.pdf#page=22) In the newer editor, `F3` starts **Add Connect** and `V` drops a Via during routing (secondary source, not verified). [Newer-editor shortcut guide](https://resources.academic.cadence.com/schematic-capture/2024-orcad-x-presto-pcb-editor-shortcut-keys) |

A **routing pair** and a **Via span** can differ on the same operation. For example, KiCad can continue a route from `F.Cu` onto `In1.Cu` using a through Via that physically reaches `B.Cu`. The leading suite chooses among the permitted physical Via types for the requested Layer change; its keypad `*` is documented as advancing to the next signal Layer. [KiCad’s routing and Via guide](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html#placing_vias) · [The leading suite’s Via placement guide](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/pads-vias)

## 4. Single-Layer and dimmed views

| Tool | Modes and controls |
|---|---|
| the leading suite | `Shift`+`S` cycles through the enabled Single Layer Modes and back to the full view. **Hide Other Layers** isolates the active Layer. **Gray Scale Other Layers** preserves brightness differences derived from each Layer colour. **Monochrome Other Layers** uses one shade for all inactive Layers. [Single Layer Mode](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/your-view-of-the-board) `Ctrl`+clicking a Layer’s eye keeps it visible in this mode, the equivalent of a REDA Pinned Layer. [Permanent visibility in Single Layer Mode](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/your-view-of-the-board) |
| KiCad 10 | The three states are normal, dimmed and hidden. In the latter two, items on inactive Layers are excluded from selection. [Appearance panel](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html#the_appearance_panel) **`H` is the three-state default in 10.0.0.** The manual’s Appearance paragraph says `Ctrl`+`H`, but its shortcut table and the released action definition both specify `H`. [Manual and shortcut table](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html) · [Released shortcut definition](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KiCad/kicad-source-mirror/10.0.0/common/tool/actions.cpp) |
| a second commercial suite | The classic editor’s **Shadow Mode** leaves the active Layer, highlighted items and items highlighted by the current command prominent. A brightness slider controls the rest, and a toolbar button toggles the mode. [Graphics Dimming or Shadow Mode, lesson 2](https://www.jotrin.com/userfiles/downloadfile/201812111502274655.pdf) A newer-editor walkthrough isolates one Layer with `Alt`+click on the floating toolbar and enables Shadow Mode separately under **Visibility → Display** (secondary source, not verified). [Routing walkthrough](https://www.ema-eda.com/courses/orcad-x-presto-walk-through/lessons/routing/) |

The editing consequences differ. KiCad’s Dim setting changes what a click can select. The leading suite can preserve a chosen reference Layer while stepping through signal Layers. The classic Shadow Mode in a second commercial suite can keep an object bright because the current command is acting on it. [KiCad’s Appearance panel](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html#the_appearance_panel) · [The leading suite’s Single Layer Mode](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/your-view-of-the-board) · [Classic Shadow Mode](https://www.jotrin.com/userfiles/downloadfile/201812111502274655.pdf)

## 5. Layer Sets and saved presets

| Tool | Built-in choices and saving a user view |
|---|---|
| the leading suite | The five supplied Layer Sets are **All Layers**, **Signal Layers**, **Plane Layers**, **NonSignal Layers** and **Mechanical Layers**. Users can save the current visibility under a new name, update it, and import or export a `.layerset` file. The `LS` control at the left end of the bottom strip recalls a set. [Layer Sets](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/your-view-of-the-board) |
| KiCad 10 | Built-ins cover all Layers, no Layers, all copper, inner copper, front, back, front assembly and back assembly. [Built-in preset definitions](https://docs.kicad.org/doxygen/appearance__controls_8cpp_source.html) **Save preset** records Layer and object visibility by name in the project settings. `Ctrl`+`Tab` opens the preset switcher. [Layer presets](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html#layer_presets) |
| a second commercial suite | The newer editor recalls predefined views from the Visibility panel or floating toolbar. The release example includes film views for individual copper Layers, solder mask and silkscreen. A user can save a named view and optionally include zoom level and board-flip state. [User-defined views, release 23.1](https://www.flowcad.de/datasheets/What-is-New-in-Release-23-1.pdf#page=6) |

The saved unit differs across tools. The leading suite’s Layer Set answers *which Layers are visible*. KiCad also remembers object visibility, such as whether Tracks are shown. A second commercial suite can recall the view from the other side of the board at a saved zoom. [Layer Set definition](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/your-view-of-the-board) · [KiCad preset contents](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html#layer_presets) · [Saved-view options](https://www.flowcad.de/datasheets/What-is-New-in-Release-23-1.pdf#page=6)

## 6. Via span labels on the canvas

| Tool | What appears at a blind or buried Via |
|---|---|
| the leading suite | **View Options → Additional Options → Via Span** puts the physical Layer numbers inside the Via. **Via Nets** controls the Net name independently; the illustrated example shows `1-6` and `GND`. [Illustrated display controls](https://files.resources.altium.com/sites/default/files/2022-07/View%20Configuration%20Panel.pdf) A blind Via’s hole also uses its two endpoint colours. For stacked Vias the displayed numbers identify the starts and ends of the Vias in the stack. [Via display guide](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/pads-vias) |
| KiCad 10 | The 10.0.0 renderer draws a hyphenated pair of physical copper ordinals on a non-through Via. Front copper is `1`, so a Via from `F.Cu` to `In1.Cu` reads `1-2`. If the Net name is visible too, the span sits above it. The two halves of a blind or buried hole use the two endpoint colours. [KiCad Via renderer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KiCad/kicad-source-mirror/10.0.0/pcbnew/pcb_painter.cpp) |
| a second commercial suite | The classic editor’s blind/buried Via labels count from `1` at the top. The Cross Section Editor can substitute a user-specified Layer ID of up to three characters, such as `Top` or `L2`. [Customizing Via Labels, p. 11](https://www.flowcad.de/AN/flowcad-cadence-AN-allegro-tips.pdf#page=11) A report for the classic editor describes **Via Labels** drawing `start : end` inside the Via Pad boundary and omitting through Vias (secondary source, not verified). [Version-specific display report](https://community.cadence.com/cadence_technology_forums/pcb-design/f/pcb-design/11975/highlighting-of-micro-buried-and-pth-vias) |

The leading suite can show the span even on a through Via. KiCad 10.0.0 reserves the span text for non-through Vias. Thus a top-to-bottom through Via on a six-Layer board can carry a `1-6` label in the former and only its Net name in the latter. [The leading suite’s Via Span option](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/your-view-of-the-board) · [KiCad’s Via label conditions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KiCad/kicad-source-mirror/10.0.0/pcbnew/pcb_painter.cpp)

## 7. Adding, removing or reordering Layers after copper exists

| Tool | Adding copper Layers | Removing a populated Layer | Reordering Layers or moving existing copper |
|---|---|---|---|
| the leading suite | Layer Stack Manager inserts beside the selected entry. Stack Symmetry can add its matching partner. Saving applies the change to the PCB editor. [Stackup editing](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/defining-layer-stack) A through Via spans the two outer Layers. A through-hole Pad belongs to **Multi-Layer**, with either common or Layer-specific Pad shapes. [Pad and Via properties](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/pads-vias) | The editor asks for confirmation when the Layer contains primitives. For a dedicated internal power Layer, the manual explicitly says confirmation removes its primitives with the Layer, including the boundaries that define its split regions. [Stackup deletion](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/defining-layer-stack) · [Internal power Layer removal](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/internal-power-split-planes) | **Move Layer Up/Down** moves the selected Layer among Layers of the same type. [Stackup editing](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/defining-layer-stack) An existing Zone with an automatically assigned name is renamed when its Layer’s stack position changes, and affected design rules are updated. A Zone can also be explicitly assigned to another Layer through its properties. [Signal-Layer Zone properties](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/signal-layer-polygons) |
| KiCad 10.0.0 | Board Setup takes a copper count and enables the corresponding numbered inner Layers between `F.Cu` and `B.Cu`. Adding Layers preserves Track assignments and Via endpoints. It also includes inner copper in the Layer membership of Footprint Pads that have a hole and are on copper. [Board Setup implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KiCad/kicad-source-mirror/10.0.0/pcbnew/dialogs/panel_setup_layers.cpp) Pad settings can still suppress an annular ring on an unconnected Layer. [Pad copper settings](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html) | The dialog lists affected items, can identify a Pad by Footprint and Pad number, and warns that continuing cannot be undone. [Removal dialog](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KiCad/kicad-source-mirror/10.0.0/pcbnew/dialogs/panel_setup_layers.cpp) A Track or single-Layer board Zone on the removed Layer is deleted. A multi-Layer board Zone retains its surviving Layers. Through Vias survive. A blind or buried Via is deleted if an **endpoint** disappears and survives if only an intermediate Layer disappears. The board-item deletion routine skips Footprints and their children. [Board-item removal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KiCad/kicad-source-mirror/10.0.0/pcbnew/board.cpp) | **Swap Layers** explicitly maps source Layers to destinations. It moves Tracks and board Zones, updates mapped endpoints on non-through Vias, and skips through Vias. Its object pass does not move Footprints or their Pads. [Swap Layers implementation](https://gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad/-/raw/10.0.0/pcbnew/tools/global_edit_tool.cpp) |
| a second commercial suite | In the classic editor, an existing through-hole padstack supplies its **DEFAULT INTERNAL** geometry on added inner Layers unless a Layer-specific definition applies. **BEGIN** and **END** correspond to the two board surfaces. [Padstack manual, lesson 3](https://www.jotrin.com/userfiles/downloadfile/201812111502274655.pdf) | A classic-editor user reports that removal was refused because the Layer contained etch. The same discussion identifies Layer-specific keepouts and Pad definitions as other possible blockers (secondary source, not verified). [Populated-Layer removal report](https://community.cadence.com/cadence_technology_forums/pcb-design/f/pcb-design/22904/how-to-remove-layers-from-existing-stack-up) | The classic editor’s **Edit → Change** moves a selected routed connection to another Layer and adds or deletes Vias as the connection requires. [Moving existing routed copper, lesson 10](https://www.jotrin.com/userfiles/downloadfile/201812111502274655.pdf) A reported workaround exchanges Tracks and text through a temporary Layer using **Change**; it establishes no automatic migration rule for Zones or Footprints (secondary source, not verified). [Layer exchange report](https://community.cadence.com/cadence_technology_forums/pcb-design/f/pcb-design/15001/swapping-layers-in-allegro-pcb/26391) |

Consider a KiCad board reduced from six copper Layers to four. `In1.Cu` and `In2.Cu` survive, while `In3.Cu` and `In4.Cu` are removed. A Track on `In3.Cu` and a blind Via ending there are deleted if the user confirms. A through Via beside them still connects the two board surfaces. This follows from the count-based Layer selection and the separate endpoint test for Vias. [KiCad Board Setup](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KiCad/kicad-source-mirror/10.0.0/pcbnew/dialogs/panel_setup_layers.cpp) · [KiCad board-item removal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KiCad/kicad-source-mirror/10.0.0/pcbnew/board.cpp)

Changing a Layer’s **type** has another consequence in the leading suite. Converting an existing signal Layer with Zones on different Nets into a dedicated power Layer loses those Zone assignments. Its knowledge base describes recreating the split regions or transferring their boundaries and reassigning Nets manually. [Signal-to-power Layer conversion](https://www.altium.com/documentation/knowledge-base/altium-designer/signal-to-plane-layer-conversion--preserving-polygons)

## What hobbyists at 4 to 6 Layers actually use

- **Four Layers.** The ULX3S FPGA board is a Zagreb Makerspace collaboration. Its published fabrication package contains separate front, `In1`, `In2` and back copper artwork. The maintainers also document isolating top or bottom paste in the Gerber viewer to count Pads for assembly. This is a recorded use of single-Layer visibility during fabrication review. [Project history](https://radiona.org/ulx3s/) · [Released artwork](https://github.com/emard/ulx3s/tree/master/plot/gerbers) · [Maintainers’ fabrication workflow](https://github.com/emard/ulx3s/tree/master/plot)
- **Six Layers.** The SS Handheld Console designer places an inner signal Layer between two ground Layers and uses it mainly for the memory buses. A separate inner Layer carries power. The design uses through Vias throughout, including Via-in-Pad under the FPGA, and publishes a layout PDF with one copper Layer per page. [Designer’s hardware notes and layout](https://github.com/badaboombox/SS-Handheld-Console/tree/main/hardware)

These records show which copper Layers, Vias and inspection views the designers used. They do not record which routing hotkeys, pair presets or dimming modes the designers used at the keyboard. [ULX3S fabrication workflow](https://github.com/emard/ulx3s/tree/master/plot) · [Handheld console hardware notes](https://github.com/badaboombox/SS-Handheld-Console/tree/main/hardware)

## Not verified / gaps

- **The leading suite.** The current references establish colour profiles, numbered Layer names and the keypad signal-Layer commands. A complete current factory palette for inner signal and power Layers, and a dedicated key to return to a saved routing-pair partner, remain unverified. [Colour preferences](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/preferences/pcb-editor) · [Routing shortcuts](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/shortcut-keys/pcb-editors)
- **The leading suite on populated boards.** The documented deletion warning does not specify the outcome for every signal-Layer Track or Zone, a placed blind Via whose endpoint disappears, an embedded Footprint, or a Pad with custom geometry on the affected Layer. Automatic relocation or repair for those cases remains unverified. [Stackup editing](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/defining-layer-stack) · [Pad and Via stack modes](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/pads-vias)
- **KiCad.** Vertical scrolling is verified for the Appearance panel. The toolbar dropdown’s exact overflow behaviour across window systems remains unverified. The detailed deletion and remapping results above are scoped to the 10.0.0 release source; preservation of individual custom Pad shapes across a remove-and-readd cycle was not established. [Appearance control](https://docs.kicad.org/doxygen/appearance__controls__base_8cpp_source.html) · [10.0.0 Layer setup](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KiCad/kicad-source-mirror/10.0.0/pcbnew/dialogs/panel_setup_layers.cpp)
- **A second commercial suite.** The floating toolbar’s overflow gesture at 16 copper Layers, factory next/previous or pair-partner keys in the newer editor, a factory Shadow Mode key, and the newer editor’s exact Via span label remain unverified. The public *User Essentials* viewer exposed its controls but no readable manual pages during this review. The 16.01 and 17.2 evidence above applies specifically to the classic editor. [Public guide viewer](https://resources.pcb.cadence.com/orcad-x-pdf-resources/orcad-x-user-essentials) · [Newer-editor release notes](https://www.flowcad.de/datasheets/What-is-New-in-Release-23-1.pdf) · [Classic 17.2 guide](https://www.flowcad.de/AN/flowcad-cadence-AN-allegro-tips.pdf)
- **A second commercial suite on populated boards.** The default inner-Pad rule and explicit Track-moving command are documented. Automatic handling of existing Tracks, Zones, Footprints, custom Pads and blind or buried Via endpoints during an in-place physical reorder in the newer editor remains unverified. Its reported dark-green default and numbered name-prefix behaviour also lack independent primary-source confirmation. [Classic Pad and routing manual](https://www.jotrin.com/userfiles/downloadfile/201812111502274655.pdf) · [Newer-editor Stackup walkthrough](https://www.ema-eda.com/how-to-page/how-to-add-layers-to-a-pcb-stackup-in-orcad/)
