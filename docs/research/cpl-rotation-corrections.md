# CPL rotation corrections

[REDA #326](https://github.com/openreda/reda/issues/326), a child of the [v0.2 map](https://github.com/openreda/reda/issues/319) · Research date 2026-11-18

Review spot-checks against the cited pages (2026-11-18): Lai’s CSV shape, row count and examples; the GPL-3.0, Apache-2.0 and AGPL-3.0 declarations for JLCKicadTools, Fabrication-Toolkit and KiBot; Fabrication-Toolkit’s fields; KiKit’s `JLCPCB_CORRECTION` field; Bouni’s bottom-side formula; Eurocircuits’ zero orientation; IEC 61188-7’s Level B preference; JLCPCB’s FAQ; the leading suite’s bottom Y-flip and centre calculation. All matched. The second commercial suite’s placement-file details rest on a third-party copy of an old manual and were not re-checked.

A placement angle has meaning only when the designer and assembler agree on the zero orientation, the side from which they view the board, and the point about which the Part turns. JLCPCB documents a mismatch between footprint and assembly-library zero. Eurocircuits publishes its own zero. AISLER derives polarity from design data. These workflows give different answers to who should correct a component before ordering. [JLCPCB assembly FAQ](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/pcb-assembly-faqs-part-2) · [Eurocircuits zero orientation](https://www.eurocircuits.com/technical-guidelines/pcb-assembly-guidelines/defining-pin-1-zero-point-rotation/) · [AISLER polarity rules](https://community.aisler.net/t/component-orientation-and-polarity-rules-for-assembly/45)

## 1. Why corrections exist

**Library zero.** For a typical multi-pin IC viewed from above, Level A puts Pin 1 at the upper left. Level B puts it at the lower left. KiCad’s library convention generally places Pin 1 at the upper left, at the left end of a two-terminal row, or at the top when Pin 1 is in the middle of a package side. Package geometry matters. Rotating every footprint by a quarter turn would also rotate packages for which the two conventions agree. Eurocircuits is a primary source for its own Level B rule and a secondary source for the comparison between standards. IEC explicitly prefers Level B for new libraries. [KiCad F4.2](https://klc.kicad.org/footprint/f4/f4.2/) · [Eurocircuits comparison and diagrams](https://www.eurocircuits.com/technical-guidelines/pcb-assembly-guidelines/defining-pin-1-zero-point-rotation/) · [IEC 61188-7:2017](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/27498)

**Revision matters.** IPC’s revision register lists IPC-7351B in June 2010 and IPC-7352 as a separate publication in July 2023. The public IPC-7351B contents identify the standard, but do not establish both A/B orientations as requirements of that revision. The account of the proposed IPC-7351C rewrite identifies the upper-left convention in B and the addition of lower-left orientation in the draft. That account is secondary evidence of the standard. The public IEC catalogue independently confirms the two-level context. A draft slide or a library guide referring to “7351C” is insufficient evidence of a published IPC-7351C. [IPC revision register](https://www.electronics.org/ipc-document-revision-table) · [IPC-7351B contents](https://www.electronics.org/TOC/IPC-7351B.pdf) · [draft history, secondary](https://www.pcblibraries.com/forum/draft-ipc7351c_topic2052.html) · [IEC catalogue](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/27498)

**Feeder zero.** EIA-481 concerns carriers for automatic handling. A manufacturer can specify the quadrant containing Pin 1 relative to the tape sprocket holes and direction of feed. TI’s ISO7760DBQR packing entry, for example, specifies Q1. That describes the physical item the feeder presents. The CAD footprint describes where numbered Pads land on the board. The assembler’s library relates the picked item to its placement model. A signed offset reconciles the two zeros when they differ. The tape drawing alone does not disclose the angle an assembler encoded for that Part. JLCPCB's assembly FAQ states the rule from the fab's side: "Zero orientation for PCB library should match the component orientation in the packaging", and warns that parts in the same package can differ by part number, so the manufacturer's tape-and-reel drawing is the thing to check. It also says the two zeros can differ while both use the same rotation direction, and that its engineers fix rotation and polarity from the silkscreen when the customer does not. [JLCPCB assembly FAQ](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/pcb-assembly-faqs-part-2) · [EIA-481-F catalogue](https://store.accuristech.com/standards/ecia-eia-481-f?product_id=2584984) · [TI packing drawing and orderable-device table](https://www.ti.com/ods/sysadd/pm/symlink/iso7761_pm.pdf) · [JLCPCB’s explanation of reel and library orientation](https://jlcpcb.com/blog/files-needed-for-pcb-assembly)

**REDA context.** REDA v0.1 writes rotation relative to the IPC-7351 zero orientation. A Fab Preset supplies `bottom_rotation` (`stored`, `plus_180`, or `negate`) and `bottom_position`. These describe an output convention for a whole side. A package or Part correction would account for a particular physical item. The v0.1 CPL has no per-package correction table. The spec places that work in v0.2. [Output specification](../spec/v0.1/outputs.md) · [Glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md)

## 3. Tools and plug-ins

In the formulas below, **a** is the placed footprint angle reported by the named tool and **d** is that tool’s signed correction. Angles are equivalent modulo 360°. For a = 30° and d = 90°, mirroring before adding the correction gives (180° − a) + d = 240°. Mirroring afterwards gives 180° − (a + d) = 60°. An offset measured in one exporter’s convention can therefore give a different placement in another. [KiBot rotation options](https://kibot.readthedocs.io/en/v1.9.1/configuration/filters/rot_footprint.html)

Each formula is in its own tool’s stored-angle convention: **a** for a bottom Footprint is whatever that tool stores after flipping, which is not necessarily the θ that REDA’s `bottom_rotation` values (`stored`, `plus_180`, `negate`) start from. This research did not establish how each tool’s stored bottom angle relates to REDA’s, so the formulas here should not be compared with the v0.1 Fab Preset values term by term.

### Library geometry, Part parameters and variants in the leading suite

**What 0° means.** The IPC Compliant Footprint Wizard generates a surface-mount land pattern from package geometry using IPC-7351B. The publisher describes IPC zero orientation in terms of the package’s Pin 1. Footprints are built as viewed from the top. A placed instance at 0° therefore retains the library drawing’s orientation. Separately, the library editor can put the footprint’s `(0, 0)` reference at Pin 1, the centre or a user-selected location. Moving that reference changes where Ref X/Y lands. It does not establish how the Part sits in a reel. [The leading suite’s wizard documentation](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/components-libraries/creating-pcb-footprint) · [the leading suite’s explanation of Pin 1](https://resources.altium.com/p/pcb-footprint-library) · [the leading suite’s explanation of library zero](https://resources.altium.com/p/pcb-land-pattern-design-ipc-7351-standard)

**What an offset parameter can do.** With `PCB.FootprintParameters` enabled, a library author can define footprint parameters that appear on the placed component and are supported in generated Pick and Place output. The publisher also documents a custom report using the BOM generator, one row per designator, with PCB parameters such as reference point and rotation. These mechanisms can carry a user-defined angle or X/Y value to a downstream process. Neither guide identifies a reserved parameter that the native exporter adds to the placement angle or coordinates. JLCPCB’s own procedure adds `LCSC Part #` for Part identification and generates the CPL through an Output Job. It specifies no per-Part correction field. [The leading suite’s parameter documentation](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/components-libraries/creating-pcb-footprint) · [the leading suite’s custom-report example](https://resources.altium.com/p/creating-a-custom-pick-and-place-file) · [JLCPCB’s export procedure](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/how-to-generate-bill-of-materials-and-centroid-file-from-altium)

**What changes with the Part.** Assembly variants can change fitted status, parameters or the selected Part, and the selection can affect Pick and Place output. If an alternate uses the same footprint name, one footprint instance is transferred to the board. If it uses a different footprint, both can be placed and positioned for their respective variants. Manufacturer Part Search can supply package information and a footprint model whose drawing has an orientation. Neither the variant guide nor the search guide documents a supplier tape quadrant or fab-specific angular correction that automatically follows an alternate Part into the CPL. [The leading suite’s variant documentation](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/design-variants) · [the leading suite’s variant output documentation](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/variants-in-design) · [the leading suite’s manufacturer-search documentation](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/components-libraries/searching-manufacturer-parts?pageId=21169091)

### Reading bottom-side data from the commercial tools

In the leading suite, the placed component’s rotation is measured CCW in degrees from the rightward horizontal. Moving a footprint to the bottom swaps its Layer-specific geometry and flips its orientation about the X axis. That editor operation and the optional export setting answer different questions. A bottom CPL row can report a rotation, a mounting side and several Y coordinates. The only values the generator explicitly promises to negate with its Y-flip option are **Ref Y and Pad Y**. The publisher gives no corresponding formula for the exported angle, no instruction to negate centre Y, and no order for applying a fab-specific angular offset. [The leading suite’s placement documentation](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/placing-components) · [the leading suite’s output documentation](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/preparing-for-manufacture/output-jobs/assembly-data)

For a second commercial suite, the **file format** matters. The classic placement text can express a 0° bottom component by retaining 0° and setting `m`. Its public file description leaves the sign convention for a particular assembler unspecified. The publisher also documents IPC-2581 manufacturing output. For IPC-2581C specifically, the format defines positive rotation as CCW viewed from board top, including bottom components, and defines a separate mirror transformation. That rule belongs to IPC-2581C. For a customized placement report, FloWare illustrates yet another explicit choice: retain top rotation and write `360° − rotation` for bottom. It is a configurable example in the extension manual. [Placement-file manual, third-party copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/228749828/Al-Gro-Place) · [the second suite’s manufacturing guide](https://resources.academic.cadence.com/schematic-capture/2025-pcb-manufacturing-files) · [IPC-2581C §§3.3.2–3.3.3, public copy](https://www.artwork.com/ipc2581/IPC-2581C.pdf) · [FloWare Variant BOM manual](https://www.flowcad.de/datasheets/FloWare-Description.pdf)

## 5. Position offsets

A footprint can have the correct Pin 1 angle and still centre the nozzle over the wrong point. The body centre, the midpoint between the outermost Pads, Pad 1 and the library anchor can all differ. KiCad illustrates an asymmetric Molex connector for which the datasheet defines the anchor. Lai’s table gives a concrete USB-C receptacle with a translation and zero angle correction. Its SOT-89 and addressable LED examples carry an angle without a translation. A package family name alone gives no reason to assign a nonzero offset. [KiCad anchor convention](https://klc.kicad.org/footprint/f6/f6.2/) · [Lai’s CSV](https://github.com/matthewlai/JLCKicadTools/blob/master/jlc_kicad_tools/cpl_rotations_db.csv) · [the leading suite’s coordinate definitions](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/preparing-for-manufacture/output-jobs/assembly-data)

The leading suite’s knowledge base gives an edge connector as a case where calculated centre X/Y can miss the body centre. The calculation uses Pads alone. For asymmetric footprints or objects without a Pad, the publisher recommends exporting Ref X/Y and setting the footprint reference point appropriately. A second commercial suite’s classic editor can also store a package-specific point. A secondary account gives the body-centre lookup order as an explicit `Body_Center` text location, then the `Place_Bound_Top` shape, then the `Dfa_Bound_Top` shape, then the pins. Both workflows can encode the intended point in the footprint without requiring an additional translation in every placement row. [The leading suite’s centroid knowledge base](https://www.altium.com/documentation/knowledge-base/altium-designer/calculate-component-pick-and-place-center-of-a-footprint) · [the second suite’s body-centre discussion, secondary](https://community.cadence.com/cadence_technology_forums/pcb-design/f/pcb-design/35912/body-center-calculation-in-export-placement)

In **footprint-local coordinates**, a displacement follows the component when it turns. A +1 mm displacement along local X points along board Y after a 90° CCW turn in a right-handed plane. KiKit explicitly uses this local convention. Bouni also transforms the vector and handles the bottom reflection. In **board coordinates**, a common X/Y shift stays in the same direction for every placement on the selected side. Eurocircuits’ whole-list alignment and MacroFab’s side-wide nudge correct that kind of registration error. A wrong drill/place origin can therefore move a whole board of otherwise correct components. [KiKit local coordinates](https://yaqwsx.github.io/KiKit/upstream/fabrication/jlcpcb/) · [Bouni exporter](https://github.com/Bouni/kicad-jlcpcb-tools/blob/main/fabrication.py) · [Eurocircuits alignment](https://www.eurocircuits.com/user-guides/visualizer-user-guides/cpl-editor-user-guide/) · [MacroFab nudge](https://www.macrofab.com/blog/announcing-updates-macrofab-pcb-placement-interfaces) · [JLCPCB origin example](https://jlcpcb.com/blog/files-needed-for-pcb-assembly)

## 6. Checking before ordering

1. **Check the data that will reach the assembler.** Select the intended BOM Part number, populated references and assembly side. Overlay the exported CPL on the exported copper and silkscreen using the same origin and units. If the whole side is displaced, inspect origin and mirroring. If only one package family is displaced, compare its anchor and Pad geometry with the package drawing. [JLCPCB file checklist](https://jlcpcb.com/blog/files-needed-for-pcb-assembly) · [Eurocircuits CPL editor](https://www.eurocircuits.com/user-guides/visualizer-user-guides/cpl-editor-user-guide/)
2. **Check orientation using a feature that breaks symmetry.** Compare the IC dot or chamfer with Pad 1/A1, a diode band with the cathode, an LED with its datasheet polarity mark, and a connector with its mating edge. Check both populated sides. JLCPCB calls the red dot an orientation mark, so its meaning must be checked for the actual Part. A centred rectangular outline can still conceal a 180° error. AISLER publishes separate rules for two-Pad Parts. [JLCPCB polarity FAQ](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/pcb-assembly-faqs-part-2) · [AISLER rules](https://community.aisler.net/t/component-orientation-and-polarity-rules-for-assembly/45) · [Eurocircuits orientation markings](https://www.eurocircuits.com/technical-guidelines/pcb-assembly-guidelines/component-orientation/)
3. **Resolve differences before production.** JLCPCB allows rotation in its preview or a new CPL upload. Its engineers default to clear silkscreen in a conflict and email if the marking is insufficient. Orders with its parts-placement confirmation option have a further production-file review. PCBWay asks for a marked drawing when the silkscreen is ambiguous and contacts the customer with questions. Eurocircuits highlights mismatches and lets the customer edit and approve individual placements or like Parts. [JLCPCB preview](https://jlcpcb.com/blog/files-needed-for-pcb-assembly) · [JLCPCB conflict policy](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/pcb-assembly-faqs-part-2) · [JLCPCB confirmation notice](https://cart.jlcpcb.com/client/template/placeOrder/component/smtConfirmFileModal.html) · [PCBWay ordering guide](https://www.pcbway.com/smt_ordering_guide.html) · [Eurocircuits editor](https://www.eurocircuits.com/user-guides/visualizer-user-guides/cpl-editor-user-guide/)
4. **Distinguish the order from the source design.** Bouni’s correction column identifies the rule used for a variant. InteractiveHtmlBom shows the design-side placement. The fab preview adds the assembler’s Part model. No inspected fab guide documents automatic write-back of a web edit into the local Footprint, schematic or exporter database. Lai documents recording the correction locally and regenerating. AISLER takes a snapshot at order time and directs customers to contact the project team for later changes. [Bouni README](https://github.com/Bouni/kicad-jlcpcb-tools) · [InteractiveHtmlBom](https://openscopeproject.org/projects/interactivehtmlbom/) · [Lai’s workflow](https://dubiouscreations.com/2019/10/21/using-kicad-with-jlcpcb-assembly-service/) · [AISLER order snapshot](https://community.aisler.net/t/rendering-guarantee-for-amazing-assembly/2427)
